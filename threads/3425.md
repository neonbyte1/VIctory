## Description

A frame budget for one fixed scene, so that performance PRs can be ranked by what they actually remove. Measured numbers only; I'll keep this updated as runs and PRs land. Others' rows for other GPUs/OSes are welcome in the same form.

**Setup:** RTX 5090 (driver 620.73.04), Linux, GE-Proton11-14 Wine with its vkd3d-proton. `main` (`63bcca4`) plus the unmerged PRs this setup needs to render the world (#2723, #439, #233, #320, #2118, #2703, #2101, #1129, #2312), plus #2958, #3120 (overlap on), #2309, #2461, #3353/#3378, #3313. GTA VI's opening prison yard, a warm shader cache, diag build. Untimed numbers are medians of the last 30 frame-rate lines of a 600 s run; timed numbers come from `VICTORY_GPU_TIMING=1` (which turns overlap off), with every command in the table rather than the log's top 25.

The title caps itself at 30 fps (#2469), so the target is 33.3 ms.

The untimed "on the GPU" figure (~240 ms) is mostly not GPU work: lists take ~176 ms of wall time, the rest is the CPU waiting on synchronous copies. With #3312 in, `GatherCopy` (41 ms) and the BVH trace (28 ms) left the top of the table.

Findings so far:
- **Clamps:** #2472's batched clamp never runs under Wine: its shader fails Wine's `D3DCompile` (`E5005 'Load' on RWByteAddressBuffer`, `[RootSignature]` ignored), so every log shows `no batched argument clamp: falling back to inline`. #3197's window shader has the same problem. A Wine-compilable version plus #3197 is being measured.
- **Write marks off** saves ~0.7 ms of GPU time on this stack: with #2958 and #3312 they're no longer a GPU cost.
- **Read marks off** isn't a usable measurement: VRAM fills to 32 GB and everything slows 2x, so it measures memory pressure, not the atomics.

A synthetic repro of the "title threads waiting to write views" row (borosf's 105 ms, neonbyte1's 74-88 ms), with A/Bs of the open PRs that touch it.

**Bench:** [`sync_contention_bench`](https://github.com/KainanP/VIctory/blob/bench/sync-heaps-contention/crates/victory-gpu/src/descriptor/sync_bench.rs) (`#[ignore]`d; commit `f34bcfb8` cherry-picks onto any tree; `cargo test -p victory-gpu --release sync_contention_bench -- --ignored --nocapture --test-threads=1`).
- A placed, write-watched view heap of 2^20 slots (capped at 934,464 by the host here).
- Each frame, one thread rewrites 200 random 64 KiB pages and runs one `sync_heaps`, while 5 title threads write a view every 20 µs into pages of their own.
- It checks that every view written before a sync is in the shader-visible view addresses after it, and that every slot is right after a final quiet sync.
- `SYNC_BENCH_*` variables change the shape. `SYNC_BENCH_SLOTS=1245184 SYNC_BENCH_PAST_CAP=1` gives the title's heap size, with pages past the cap.

On `main` it reproduces the shape: a 9.6 ms sync leaves each title thread blocked for all of it, so the threads wait **49.5 ms per frame** in total (per-write p99 9.7 ms, against 2-3 µs uncontended).

On the "Descriptor heap mirroring (384k slots), 18.1 ms, `HostHeap::sync_slots`, none found" row: I wrote a bench in that shape and ran the open PRs through it. I also covered borosf's "heap slots the title copied" row (493,568 slots, 36.6 ms), which goes through the same `mirror`.

**Bench:** [`bench/sync-slots`](https://github.com/KainanP/VIctory/commits/bench/sync-slots) (`f233390e` on top of `f34bcfb8`; cherry-pick both, `#[ignore]`d).
- `sync_slots_bench`: each frame, 192 random 64 KiB pages (393,216 slots) of a 2^20-slot placed view heap are written, with 0, ~9.5k or ~85k records changed, then one `sync_heaps`.
- `copy_simple_bench`: 393,216 slots a frame copied with `copy_simple` in runs of 1-1024.
- Every shape runs with and without 5 title threads writing views.
- Both check every view address afterwards.
- Run: `cargo test -p victory-gpu --release sync_slots_bench -- --ignored --nocapture --test-threads=1`.

**Where the time goes on `main`** (RTX 4060 Laptop):
- Scanning 393k unchanged slots is cheap: ~1.2 ms for the record diff, plus ~1.7 ms for the write-watch/page walk.
- Each changed slot's `mirror` costs ~1 µs. ~9.5k changed per frame gives 12-17 ms, close to the 18.1 ms row, so the row is most likely `mirror` of changed slots, not the scan.
- `copy_simple` of records the slots already hold costs 71 ns/slot here, the same as borosf's 74 ns/slot, because each slot takes `SHADER_HEAPS` and the slot locks on its own.

- the copy is write-watched.
