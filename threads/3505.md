# Marvisak/VIctory#3505: 8 GB NVIDIA laptop data: VICTORY_MEMORY_CARD_MB=2048 was worth ~6x, plus a possible VRAM creep

- Issue by @YUYING234, opened 2026-11-27T11:16:24Z, state **open**
- <https://github.com/Marvisak/VIctory/issues/3505>
- Archived copy; the original thread is authoritative while it exists.

## Description

Measured on an 8 GB NVIDIA **laptop** (RTX 4060 Laptop, 8188 MiB; D3D12 budget reported 6489-6569 MB), i9-14900HX, 32 GB DDR5, Windows 11 build 26300, driver 621.01, hybrid graphics. Public fork `edsuuu/VI-BR-PORT`, built from source (MSVC 14.44 + rust 1.98.1, ~45 s). No game files included or linked.

Posting here because two of the findings look layer-level rather than fork-level, and this is where most people will look.

## Results (Series S profile, 1080p)

- Menus 30 fps (title cap), cell 17-24, walking 12-17, heavy 8.5k-draw street 9-12
- 6 min 11 s session, 47 samples: median **12 fps**, GPU median 85 %, 62-64 C, no throttling
- Frame rate is nearly independent of draw count here (3k draws -> 13 fps, 8.5k -> 12 fps): the cost is per-frame fixed overhead, not scene scale

## Two switches that mattered

| Change | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `VICTORY_MEMORY_CARD_MB=1024` -> `2048` | **1-3 fps** (GPU only 3-25 % busy, frames 300-1200 ms) | **13-17 fps** (GPU 85 %) |
| `VICTORY_HEAP_RECHECK=0` | prepare 44 ms/frame, copies 12 ms / 70 MB | prepare **29 ms**, copies **10 ms / 55 MB** |

The first one is worth ~6x on this card: at 1024 the GPU sits idle most of the frame, waiting for memory. The second changes no frame rate while the GPU is the limit, but removes about a third of the host-side work, so it should matter as the GPU side improves. No visual difference; the "texture picked by index changed after its upload" warnings drop to zero.

## Negative results (so nobody repeats them)

- 720p output instead of 1080p: 13-17 -> 13-14 fps. Not pixel-bound.
- `VICTORY_ASYNC_SUBMIT=1`: 13-17 -> 7-12 fps on this 32 GB laptop, i.e. worse (it is reported as +1.4 fps on a 64 GB desktop, so it looks memory/thread-count dependent).
- Half render scale and FSR: no change, same reason as 720p.

## Something that may be a real leak

VRAM goes 7342 -> 7921 MiB in 6 minutes (96 % of 8192). At the ceiling the frame rate collapses to 3-4 fps and textures blur; restarting the title resets it. If this reproduces for others on 8 GB, a note about session length would save people some confusion.

## Shaders that still fail to translate (dropped draws)

`002e93a6cc2aa355`, `0e64f14fae060d04`, `dbdbf36298727135`, `96ccb14ef981fd85` (a lane-read VGPR written without a dword value); `77360df21e12ea43`, `9fc111d3be79364f`, `39321a95223001ed` (a first-lane read where not every lane); `07c93bfb77fecebb` (a descriptor read only as data). Plus 7 compute pipelines refused by the driver with `E_INVALIDARG (0x80070057)`.

Full configuration and the A/B tables: `edsuuu/VI-BR-PORT` PR #7 (`docs/benchmarks-4060-laptop-8gb.md`). Tracker entry: 4lwayz/portwatch-vi issue #251.

## The answer to your question

**In the world, yes: this base is GPU-bound on this card — but with a heavy CPU tail.** In the last four samples the GPU holds **110 to 163 ms of a 175 to 230 ms frame (55-75 %)**, with 33-45 ms preparing (12-17 ms of that copies) and 23-30 ms copying back 21-29 MB. In the lighter interior (1,574 draws) the picture flips: GPU 28-47 % busy and one **submit thread at 41-57 %** becomes the limit, which is the same shape as VI-BR-PORT on this card.

Two further numbers that may matter more than the fps: **234 to 396 draws a frame are dropped without a host pipeline** once the world is up, and RAM reaches **19.2-22.9 GB in the process, 30.2 of 32 GB for the system** — this laptop has no headroom left at that point.
