## Description

Refs #3425.

### What this does

`Tracked::take` and `Tracked::taken` hold `watch::GUARDS` while `protect_noting` calls `VirtualProtect` on every run they take. The write-fault handler needs the same lock. #3425 measures title threads waiting about 52 ms/frame in the handler behind it (RTX 5090/Wine), and borosf's Arc B580 numbers there show 76 ms/frame. I found no open PR that moves the protection change itself out of the lock. #3343 and #2849 batch the unprotect/protect calls, #3035 moves the saved-bytes read, and #3455 turns `GUARDS` into an RwLock (the protect still runs under the write lock).

With `VICTORY_GUARD_OUTSIDE=1`, a take notes its runs as guarded under the lock (`Guards::arming`). It changes their protection after releasing the lock (`Arming::finish`) and before its caller copies them. **Off by default**: without the variable the code path is the same as before, apart from an `is_empty()` check.

This ports the idea from our fork's [fxceless/victory-fork#1315](https://github.com/fxceless/victory-fork/pull/1315). The code there doesn't map onto this one (the fork guards 64 KiB blocks with a journal, while VIctory has `busy` and `alias_of`), so this is a rewrite, not a cherry-pick.

### Why the answers stay the same

- **Concurrent takes wait.** A take that reaches a page another take is still arming waits on a condvar (`ARMED`). Otherwise it could count the page as guarded, copy it, and miss a write that lands before the guard is in place.
- **Pages with another mapping stay under the lock.** Runs that include a page shown at another mapping (`alias_of`) are guarded under the lock along with their other mappings, as before. `title_memory_aliased` waits for armed pages before linking them.
- **Pages opened while armed end up writable.** If a fault, `writing` or a remap opens a page while it is being armed, the page is marked changed as before and noted as `disturbed`. `finish` then makes it writable again. A stale fault on such a page (the thread faulted before the opening and retries after `finish`'s change) is let through by the handler.
- **Pages that can't be made read-only end as they did under the lock.** They are taken as zeros if nothing is mapped. Otherwise they stay changed and keep the bytes their tracker had saved.

### Tests (CPU only, `watch.rs`)

- `refreshes_under_writes_lose_nothing`: five copies (two overlapping copies of a write-watched pool, plus copies of both mappings of twin views) are refreshed together on separate threads while 4 writers write. It checks that every word written before a refresh is in every copy covering it, and that a quiet refresh afterwards leaves each copy equal to title memory. A test-only spin before the out-of-lock protect widens the window a preempted take would leave.
  - I checked that it fails if the condvar wait is removed (5/5 runs, by round 2) or if the `alias_of` check is removed (5/5 runs, by round 7, with "copy 2 lost a write").
  - Without the handler's `disturbed` branch the process crashes with an access violation.
- `armed_pages_opened_or_unmapped_end_as_under_the_lock`
- `guard_contention` (`#[ignore]`, benchmark): `cargo test -p victory-gpu --release --lib guard_contention -- --ignored --nocapture`, with sizes set by `BENCH_*` variables (see its doc).
- The full `victory-gpu --lib` suite also passes with `VICTORY_GUARD_OUTSIDE=1`: WARP with `--features diagnostics`, 248/248. On an RTX 4060 Laptop, the only failures are the two that also fail on `main` there (the 1M view-heap cap: `dynamic_slots_are_held_until_released`, `lists_share_a_table_and_copies_keep_their_views`), plus a timing flake in `descriptor::host` that shows up in both modes.
- clippy `-D warnings` passes both default and `--all-features`, and xtask policy passes.

### Measurements (honest version: no win in the synthetic)

`guard_contention` runs on a 256 MB write-watched pool split into 32-page copies. Before each refresh 1,400 pages are written, then writers keep writing random words every 20 µs during the refresh. Off and on alternate within one process, 31 rounds, medians. Machine: 24 threads, about 11–40 % foreign load (another build running).

| config | refresh off → on | writers' time in their writes off → on | writers' wait for `GUARDS` off → on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 writer | 21.5 → 23.8 ms | 7.48 → 7.37 ms | **3.76 → 0.05 ms** |
| 4 writers | 34.0 → 40.4 ms | 39.9 → 44.9 ms | 25.5 → 19.4 ms |
| 4 writers, refresh on 4 threads | 10.3 → 13.5 ms | 32.9 → 38.3 ms | 30.3 → 26.8 ms |
| 4 writers, twin views (all runs stay under the lock: A/A control) | 32.2 → 32.6 ms | 59.8 → 59.1 ms | 41.8 → 41.0 ms |

- **The lock wait behind takes goes away.** With one writer, the guard-lock wait drops from 3.76 to 0.05 ms.
- **But the faulting write doesn't get faster.** A faulting write still costs about 20–30 µs, and the total time writers spend in writes stays the same. The time seems to move into the kernel's own protection changes, which most likely serialize there: the take's read-only change and the fault's read-write change both modify this process's mappings.
- **What's left with 4 writers** is mostly writers waiting on each other's handlers, which call `VirtualProtect` (open) under the lock. This PR doesn't touch that path.
- **Refresh gets slower** (+10–30 %), because each take now takes the lock twice and waits behind handlers the second time.

This matches what we saw in the fork. There, on a 5080, guard waits went from 23 to 0.5 ms with **no change in frame time** ([fxceless/victory-fork#1379](https://github.com/fxceless/victory-fork/issues/1379)). The synthetic doesn't model the title's real write pattern, though, and #3425's profile puts about 49 ms/frame of `GUARDS` hold time on the takes. So the only real test is in-game, which is why this is a draft and off by default.

**A/B request.** @neonbyte1 (5090/Wine) and @borosf (Arc B580): could you run the prison-yard scene with and without `VICTORY_GUARD_OUTSIDE=1`, with the `diagnostics` build so `held_times`, `GuardWaitUs`, `HandlerUs` and `ProtectUs` are in the log?
